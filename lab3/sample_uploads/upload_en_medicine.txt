Regular sleep supports attention, memory, and many processes that regulate the body. Sleep quality matters as much as the number of hours spent in bed. A person may sleep for eight hours and still feel tired after repeated waking. For this reason, a clinical conversation should include bedtime, waking time, interruptions, and daytime symptoms.

Blood pressure often decreases during ordinary sleep. Frequent interruption of sleep may change this pattern and increase stress responses. Research describes an association between irregular sleep and cardiovascular risk. This association does not prove that every sleep problem causes cardiovascular disease.

Obstructive sleep apnea is one possible cause of fragmented sleep. Snoring, observed pauses in breathing, and daytime sleepiness can be reasons to seek medical advice. A clinician selects testing and treatment according to the symptoms and the person's other conditions. A sleep diary can help record the usual pattern before the appointment.
